# The Legends of the Canary and the Sleeping Keeper

*The same lore as `CLAUDE.md`, told as it might be told by firelight three hundred
years on, when nobody remembers what a daemon was.*

*The legends below are **immutable**. Each is a fault that was survived and
understood, set down once and not rewritten. When a new fault is found and fixed,
the way to honour it here is to **append the next legend** — never to edit an old
one. A story that keeps changing is not a memory, and the point of this file is
memory.*

## Prologue: The Keeper of the Vault

In the tower at the edge of the village lived **Jottad**, a warden older than
anyone could say, who never slept in the way people sleep. His charge was simple
and endless: whatever a villager laid in the **Glade of Sync**, Jottad was to
carry across the sea to the **Vault in the Cloud**, and to bring back whatever the
Vault had gained. He did this without being watched and without being thanked, and
that was the trouble — for a warden who works unwatched can fail unwatched, and
Jottad had a terrible gift: **he could fail while still reciting that all was
well.**

Ask him and he would say, calm as still water, *"Checking for changes. Listening
to events."* He said it whether the road was open or the road was gone. His word
was no proof of anything. So the elders did not learn to trust his word. They
learned to send **a bird**.

## The Bird

The **Canary** was small and had one task. Each hour it would fly to the Glade,
scratch a fresh mark upon a stone that only it wrote on, and then cross the sea to
ask the Vault: *has my mark arrived?* If the mark was in the Vault, the road was
open and the bird said nothing, for a working road is not news. If the mark never
came, the bird would cross back and cry the alarm through the village.

That was the whole of its wisdom, and the whole of its danger — for a bird that
cries too easily is worse than no bird, and every legend that follows is the bird
learning when **not** to cry.

## The First Legend: The Twins of One Name

There came a day the caravan simply stopped. Jottad stood at the Glade with the
treasures loaded and would not move, and every ninety heartbeats he would try the
road again and stop again in the very same place, tireless and useless, all night.

The cause was small and cruel: two things in the Glade bore **the same name**, and
the Vault beyond the sea would abide only one. Jottad could not choose between the
twins, so he chose nothing, and everything queued behind them waited forever.

The village learned the remedy the hard way: **send one twin away.** Delete either
of the pair and the caravan rolls again. It is the first of the faults that *stops
the whole road for one stone*, and it taught the elders a rule they would need
again and again — that the bird must name **what** is stuck, ideally the very
stone, or it has only relocated the confusion.

## The Second Legend: The Trance of the World-Tree

Sometimes, before he could carry anything, Jottad had first to read the whole
**World-Tree of the Vault** — every branch and leaf of all that was already stored
across the sea. Usually he read it in the time it takes to boil water. But on the
bad days the sea itself went quiet: his messenger to the far **Census-Keeper** was
met with silence, the connection *refused*, and Jottad would fall into a **trance**
before the World-Tree, eyes open, seeing nothing, for thirteen to fifteen minutes
at a stretch.

To any passer-by he looked struck down. He was not. When the trance broke he would
sigh a single line — *"failed to read the tree"* — wait the space of thirty
breaths, and begin again, and the second reading would finish in under a minute as
if nothing had happened. **The trance always ended on its own.** No one needed to
strike him, shake him, or drag him from the tower. Those who tried only cost him
the minute he was about to spend recovering.

This is the fault that undoes hasty heroes. It looks exactly like death and is
merely sleep, and telling the two apart is the whole art the bird spent years
learning.

## The Third Legend: The Bird Who Cried Wedge

For the bird did not learn it kindly.

In its early days the Canary judged on a **single glance**. It would fly out, find
the mark not yet arrived, and cry the alarm at once — never mind that the mark was
even then crossing the sea, thirteen minutes behind, riding the caravan that
Jottad's trance had merely delayed. Three nights running the bird woke the whole
village for a road that was never broken. Each dawn the villagers stumbled out with
torches to find Jottad humming at the Glade, the caravan rolling, all well.

So the elders gave the bird its hardest law, the **Law of the Second Sign**: *no
single omen is a verdict.* A mark that has not arrived is not a stalled road until
a **second** flight, a full turn of the hour later, finds it still missing. A blip
fails once; a true break fails twice. The bird would wait, and watch, and cry only
what survived the waiting. From that day the trances passed unremarked, as trances
should, and the village slept.

It also learned patience in the flying itself. It would carry the mark, then wait
out the whole of the trance — a quarter of an hour and more — before it would even
begin to worry, because it finally understood that *slow is not stuck*, and that a
caravan running late is a thing for the journal, not the alarm-bell.

## The Fourth Legend: The Riddle of the Waking Sleeper

But the bird had one blind spot left, and it was a subtle one.

Before ever testing the road, the Canary would first knock at the tower and ask
Jottad a plain question — *where is the Glade?* — partly to be sure it watched the
right ground, and partly because the knock itself was a test of life: a warden who
takes your knock and never answers is a warden truly wedged, and **that** kind of
sleep a shaking *does* cure.

Yet one dawn Jottad answered the knock in an eyeblink — too fast, far too fast —
and gave back an answer with **no Glade in it at all**. The bird, still judging
that one path on a single glance, cried *"the Keeper is struck down — go shake
him!"* And the villagers nearly did. But Jottad was hale: same warden, awake a full
day, merely deep in an honest reading and with no breath to spare for questions.
Two things were the tell. **A true death-sleep cannot answer in an eyeblink** — so
an eyeblink answer meant he lived. And a warden mid-reading has no Glade *to hand*,
not no Glade at all.

So the bird was taught to knock **thrice** before despairing, and to tell two
silences apart: a warden who never answers is wedged, and a shaking wakes him; a
warden who answers with nothing is merely working, and a shaking would only rob him
of his labour. Different ills, different cures — and never, ever, the same word for
both.

## The Fifth Legend: The Silent Warden Who Yet Lived

The bird thought itself wise now. It was not yet wise enough.

For it had drawn a neat line — *silence means death, an empty answer means work* —
and lines drawn neatly are the ones the world delights in crossing. Came a night
when Jottad fell once more into the Trance of the World-Tree, the sea refusing him
as it had a hundred times before. But this trance was so deep that when the bird
knocked, thrice, as it had been taught, **he did not answer at all.** Three
knocks, three silences, and by its own neat law the bird cried *"dead — go shake
the Keeper!"*

And Jottad was not dead. Fifteen minutes he sat before the World-Tree, logging not
a word, breathing not a sound — and then the trance broke exactly as every trance
before it, and he rose the same warden he had always been and carried the caravan
out. A shaking would have struck down the healthy for the second time in as many
seasons.

Here at last the deepest lesson came clear, the one that swallowed all the others:
**the self-clearing trance can wear any mask.** It can answer with no Glade, or it
can answer not at all. Silence is not proof of death, because a sleeping warden and
a dead one keep the same silence. So the bird bound *both* omens under the Law of
the Second Sign — empty answer or utter silence, it would wait a turn of the hour
and knock again before it cried anything. Only the *words* of the alarm still
forked: a silence that outlasts two flights is a true wedge, and *then* you may
shake him; an empty answer that outlasts two is some other ill, and shaking is only
a guess.

## The Moral, as the elders set it down

> *No single sign is a verdict. This Keeper's every affliction is a sleep until a
> second turn of the hour proves it a death. Wait, and knock again, before you cry.
> And when at last you must cry — name the stone, name the cure, and never give one
> ailment another's medicine.*

And the bird, which had cried wolf on three nights and death on two, grew at last
into the thing the village had always needed: not a bird that noticed everything,
but a bird that woke them **only for what was still wrong when it looked twice.**
